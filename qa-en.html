After leaving the main path and descending into a wet field, how many times does Udayaditya's horse, Sugriva, fall onto its front legs?

Answer

Three times.

Rationale

The text states: "After covering a distance of five kroshas, the Yubaraj left the path and descended into a field... As it went, the horse fell three times, putting its weight on its front legs." Shortly after, he encourages the horse by calling its name, "Sugriva!"

What is the emotional significance of the song 'I alone am left behind,' and in what two contrasting contexts does Basanta Ray sing it?

Answer

The song represents Basanta Ray's profound loneliness and grief over losing his loved ones. He first sings it alone in Raigarh while watching the sunset, feeling isolated, and later sings it directly to Vibha in Jessore upon seeing Surma's empty room and realizing she has died.

How does the symbolism of the window change for Udayaditya between his time in his bedchamber after Surma's death and his time in the prison cell?

Answer

In his bedchamber, the window is a place of grief where he stares at the empty spot where Surma used to sit. In his prison cell, the window (casement) becomes his only source of hope; he sleeps on the floor next to it so he can look at the sky and birds and dream of freedom.

Rationale

In Chapter 25, Vibha finds him sleeping on the prison floor by the casement rather than his bed. He explains, "If I sit on the cot, I can't see the sky... I forget that I will one day be free," using the window to visualize breaking his cage.

How does Sitaram's strategy involving the guardhouse fire evolve from a simple distraction into a full-fledged cover-up of Udayaditya's escape?

Sitaram and his men initially start a fire near the guard huts to create a distraction, allowing Sitaram to open the prison door and sneak Udayaditya out to a boat. Despite Udayaditya's protective actions during the midnight assassination attempt, how does Ramchandra Ray later interpret his brother-in-law's behavior in his own court?

Answer

Answer

Pratapaditya angrily rejects his minister's warnings that executing Basanta Ray will anger the Emperor of Delhi and his fifty thousand soldiers. How does the grooming of Vibha's hair serve as a marker of Surma's presence and subsequent tragic absence in the Jessore palace?

Answer

While Surma is alive, she acts as a loving confidant and restyles Vibha's hair to the girl's liking after the Mahishi ruins it with heavy styling. Following Surma's suicide, a grieving Vibha sits with unbraided hair, endlessly waiting for Surma to come fix it, leading Rammohan to later point out her unkempt hair as proof of her neglect.

Rationale

In Chapter 8, Vibha secretly has Surma restyle her hair because her mother's styling is unbearable. In Chapter 18, after Surma dies, Vibha sits quietly with her hair unbraided, hopelessly feeling as though Surma will come at any moment to braid it. In Chapter 23, Rammohan arrives and points to her unkempt hair as a physical sign that she is suffering and uncared for.
